The Basics

What is OSINT?

Open Source Intelligence (OSINT) is the practice of collecting information from publicly available sources — social media, public records, websites, and photos. Criminals use these exact techniques to profile their targets before launching attacks, without touching a piece of malware.

Your Digital Footprint

Five ways you're exposed

These are the five primary categories of data that attackers can find and exploit about you.

Social Media Data

Profile details, tagged photos, check-ins, and daily routines shared across Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok.

Professional Data

LinkedIn profiles revealing your role, colleagues, and projects. Company websites listing staff names and email formats.

Photo & File Metadata

Every photo on your phone stores hidden GPS coordinates, device model, and timestamp without you knowing.

Exposed Credentials

Your email and password may already be in a public breach database. One old breach can unlock multiple accounts.

Technical & Domain Data

WHOIS records and DNS lookups can reveal your hosting provider and sometimes your personal contact details.

Real Cases

It happens to real people

These are real, publicly documented cases where OSINT exposed individuals in ways they never expected.

John McAfee — Location Exposed Through Photo Metadata

In 2012, antivirus pioneer John McAfee was hiding from authorities in Guatemala. A journalist from Vice Magazine photographed him and published the image online. The photo's embedded EXIF metadata contained GPS coordinates that revealed his exact location. He was found within hours.

Bellingcat — Identifying War Criminals Through OSINT

Investigative outlet Bellingcat used entirely open source techniques — social media posts, satellite imagery, and public records — to identify the soldiers responsible for shooting down flight MH17 in 2014. No hacking was involved, only publicly available information.

Russian Trolls — Exposed Through Digital Footprints

In 2018, researchers identified Russian Internet Research Agency operatives running fake social media accounts by cross-referencing usernames, profile photos, and posting patterns across multiple platforms — all publicly available data.

    What You Can Do

    Protect yourself

    Small changes to your online habits can significantly reduce your exposure.

    Social Media

    • Audit your privacy settings on every platform
    • Remove your date of birth and phone from public profiles
    • Be cautious about tagging your real-time location

    Photos & Files

    • Disable location services for your camera app
    • Strip EXIF data before sharing photos publicly
    • Remove metadata from documents before uploading

    Passwords & Accounts

    • Check haveibeenpwned.com for your email addresses
    • Use a unique strong password for every account
    • Enable two-factor authentication everywhere

    Professional Presence

    • Think carefully about what your LinkedIn reveals
    • Avoid listing internal tools or project names publicly
    • Make your connections list private
